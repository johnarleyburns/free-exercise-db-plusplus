# v0.4 complex-movement mapping notes

This batch includes the v0.3.2 exact-name cleanup plus v0.4 complex movement families.

## Evidence model

Complex movements remain `medium` confidence even when explicitly recognized. Their
muscle roles are whole-exercise bookkeeping approximations across multiple phases,
not claims that every repetition provides a full hypertrophy-equivalent set to every
listed direct muscle.

### Olympic weightlifting

The clean and snatch use coordinated, rapid hip/knee/ankle extension. Recent
biomechanical/EMG research reports substantial activity in vastus lateralis,
gluteals, biceps femoris, and gastrocnemius, while NSCA's position statement
emphasizes rapid forceful hip/knee/ankle extension in the second pull.

DB++ therefore treats quadriceps + glutes as direct in clean/snatch families,
traps as direct during pull-dominant phases, hamstrings/calves as indirect, and
the trunk/forearms plus overhead receiving musculature primarily as stabilizers
unless an exercise includes a true pressing/jerk phase.

References:
- Arauz et al. J Biomech. 2026. PMID 41352184.
- Comfort et al. J Strength Cond Res. 2023. PMID 36952649.
- Kipp et al. J Appl Biomech. 2012. PMID 21975459.

### Kettlebell ballistic movements

EMG studies characterize swing, clean, and snatch as total-body exercises with
meaningfully different activation patterns. Swing research also reports large
gluteal and erector-spinae activation with a hip-hinge/squat pattern.

DB++ therefore uses hip extensors as primary direct muscles for clean/snatch
ballistics, with exercise-specific shoulder/trap/quadriceps contributions and
trunk/forearm stabilization.

References:
- Lyons et al. J Strength Cond Res. 2017. PMID 28394829.
- McGill & Marshall. J Strength Cond Res. 2012. PMID 21997449.

### Strongman

Strongman events are modeled as event families rather than conventional single
joint patterns. Atlas stone research describes distinct grip, first-pull, lap,
and second-pull phases and similarities to Romanian deadlift/front-squat
mechanics. Tire flip research explicitly describes pull, transition, and push
phases. Strongman trunk-loading research confirms substantial whole-body/trunk
demands across stones, carries, tire flips, and log lifts.

DB++ therefore credits lower-body prime movers directly for stone/load/tire
families, while treating arm/trap/press contributions according to the event's
phase structure.

References:
- Hindle et al. 2021. Atlas stone biomechanics. PMID 34557349.
- Keogh et al. J Strength Cond Res. 2010. Tire flip. PMID 20386131.
- McGill et al. J Strength Cond Res. 2009. Strongman trunk loading. PMID 19528856.
- Hindle et al. Sports Med Open systematic review. PMID 31820223.

## Confidence policy

- `high`: reviewed simple/isolation or explicit established conventional mapping.
- `medium`: recognized compound or complex family.
- `low`: unresolved fallback.

Complex families use review reason `reviewed_complex_pattern_rule` so outside
reviewers can filter them separately from ordinary rule-based compound mappings.
